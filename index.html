Anatomy of a viral launch

Social Capital promises five million views on any product launch. I pulled every launch they list publicly to see how they actually do it. Nine launches, 97 posts, one pattern.

First

The post goes out from the founder, not the company.

Seven of the nine launches open with a post on the founder's personal account. Not the brand handle, not a press release. A person, a video, and usually a funding number.

Hover a row for the post.

Second

It goes out on a Monday, before lunch in New York.

Eight of nine hero posts land on a Monday or Tuesday. All nine go live between 13:00 and 18:00 UTC. That is mid-morning on the US east coast and evening in India, and it leaves the rest of the week for what comes next.

Third

Then someone tells the founder's life story.

Within a day, a post appears that says nothing about the product. It is the founder's biography, written as greentext, and it usually beats every product post in the wave.

Fourth

The same people show up every time.

Eight accounts appear in more than one launch. One of them, @kritarthmittal, appears in seven of nine and wrote the biography in five. Amber marks the launches where an account wrote the greentext.

Two accounts, one brief. From the Cartesia launch, 46 hours apart.

Fifth

And the wave comes back on day five.

A launch is not a day. After the first burst the timeline goes quiet, then on day five a second set of posts lands almost together. In the Wispr Flow launch, eight accounts posted inside three hours, four days after the founder.

The result

Reach that has nothing to do with follower count.

Divide a hero post's views by its author's followers. A launch on its own usually gets 5 to 50 views per follower. Social Capital launches from accounts with under 40,000 followers get 100 to 400. That gap is the network.

So

They launch the founder, not the product.

A founder's account, an early-week slot, a biography instead of a review, a roster that repeats, and a second wave when everyone else has moved on. None of it is visible from any single post. All of it is visible from nine.

How this was made, and what it cannot show

Launch list

The nine case studies on sociallcapital.com/work as of 15 September 2026. Each page embeds one hero post, and that post anchors everything else.

Numbers

From the public fxtwitter API, which returns views, likes, replies, reposts, bookmarks, author followers and video length without a login. Fetched on , so older launches have had longer to accumulate.

Wave posts

Found with X search limited to a date window after each hero post, the product or founder name, and 20 or more likes. X returns one reliable page per query, so each wave is a sample of the most engaged posts, not a census. Wispr Flow also merges URLs from a public dataset another applicant compiled (waveline-socap); every post was re-fetched here.

Comparison set

Nine AI launch posts from company or founder accounts with no known agency: Cluely, Manus, Devin and Cursor. Picked for being well known, not for matching size, which is why views per follower is the fair measure.

Founder biography

A post written as greentext lines starting with "be", the 4chan life-story format. Detected by pattern, then read by hand.

What this cannot show

Whether any account was paid. Which posts were written in-house. LinkedIn, where Social Capital says most of its reach is, is not publicly searchable. The roster overlap and the biography pattern are visible in public data; the mechanism behind them is inferred.
