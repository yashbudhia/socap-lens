Public data study, built for the Social Capital technical generalist application

Launch Ledger

Every launch Social Capital Inc lists publicly, its hero post, and the creator wave around it. Live numbers from public X data, a comparison set of nine launches that did not use them, and one thing the data says about how they actually work.

The finding

Social Capital does not amplify the product. It amplifies the founder's life story, on a schedule, through a small recurring roster.

The hero post is on the founder's personal account in 7 of 9 launches. The highest-reach posts in the wave are not product reviews. They are greentext biographies of the founder ("be Karan Goel, spawn in Delhi") posted by the same handful of Indian tech-Twitter accounts, and one account, @kritarthmittal, shows up in 7 of the 9 launches. The wave is bimodal: a lore burst in the first 24 hours and a second, product-angle burst on day four, often landing within a three hour window.

    Method and limits

    • Launch list. The nine case studies on sociallcapital.com/work as of 15 Sep 2026. Each page embeds one hero post; that post ID is the anchor for everything else.
    • Metrics. Pulled from the public fxtwitter API (api.fxtwitter.com/status/ID), which returns views, likes, replies, reposts, quotes, bookmarks, author followers, and media duration without authentication. Numbers are as of the generated timestamp above, so older launches have had longer to accumulate.
    • Wave posts. Found with X search restricted to a date window after each hero post, the product or founder name, and a minimum of 20 likes. X only reliably returns the first page of results per query, so each launch's wave is a sample of the most engaged posts, not a census. Wispr Flow additionally merges URLs from a public dataset another applicant compiled (github.com/atzgg132/waveline-socap); every post was re-fetched and re-verified here.
    • Comparison set. Nine AI product launch posts from company or founder accounts with no known agency involvement: Cluely, Manus (two), Devin (three), Cursor (three). Chosen for being well known, not for being similar in size. Views per follower is the fair comparison; raw views are not.
    • What "founder lore" means. A post whose body starts with greentext lines ("> be X"). Detected by regex, then read by hand.
    • What this cannot show. Whether any wave account was paid. Which posts were written in-house versus by the creator. LinkedIn, where Social Capital claims most of its reach, is not publicly searchable. The founder-lore pattern and the roster overlap are visible in public data; the mechanism behind them is inferred.
    • Code. Static site plus three small Node scripts. No framework. Source on GitHub: yashbudhia/socap-lens.
